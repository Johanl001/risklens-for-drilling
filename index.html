The problem

NPT is where the money and the risk both live

Non-Productive Time typically consumes roughly a fifth to a quarter of drilling time industry-wide. It is concentrated in a small set of recurring, largely preventable events — which is exactly the kind of pattern real-time risk intelligence is built to catch early.

The proposal

Three modules, extending RiskLens into well delivery

Each module is a real-time analytics and alerting layer — advisory, human-in-the-loop, and deployable without touching rig control hardware.

How the system works — data flow
A software-only pipeline — no rig hardware changes required. The AI engine sits between the existing WITSML data stream and the driller's console.
SP‑01

Stuck-pipe early warning

Real-time ML on torque, drag and hookload trends flags a rising probability of sticking before it happens, using self-adjusting baselines rather than fixed thresholds — so it works across different rigs and formations without retraining from scratch.

WC‑02

Well-control early warning

Anomaly detection on flow-in/flow-out and standpipe pressure catches kicks and losses earlier, while cutting the false-alarm rate that causes alarm fatigue and slows crew response on the rig floor.

RC‑03

NPT root-cause analytics

NLP applied to historical daily drilling reports turns months of unstructured, inconsistently-worded logs into a searchable, structured record of what actually causes NPT — well by well, rig by rig, basin by basin.

Implementation approach

How this would actually get built

01

Data & sensor audit

Map what's already streaming from each rig — WITSML feeds, mud logging, existing alarms — before designing anything new.

02

Historical tagging & training

Label past NPT events against historical drilling parameters to train the stuck-pipe and well-control models.

03

Pilot, advisory mode

Run on one or two rigs as an alert layer only — no automated control — while drillers validate the recommendations.

04

Measure & scale

Compare NPT hours and false-alarm rates against baseline before expanding to more rigs or basins.

The main risk isn't the modeling — it's data quality and driller trust. Starting in advisory mode, with transparent reasoning behind every alert, is what makes adoption realistic.
Why this fits SwarmLens specifically

A software layer, not a hardware bet

RiskLens already turns real-time operational data into risk identification and faster decisions. This proposal doesn't reinvent that — it points the same approach at drilling, where the highest-value use cases (early warning, not autonomous rig control) are achievable as software, without the rig-floor hardware integration that only large oilfield service companies can realistically deploy.
Scope positioning — where this proposal sits
The deliberate scope choice — staying in the software advisory zone — is what makes this achievable for a small team without oilfield-service hardware partnerships.

That's a deliberate scoping choice: it's the kind of first product a small, fast-moving team could actually ship and pilot with a client — not a moonshot that depends on someone else's equipment.
